## Thresholds and release checks

For each national series, form the 28 absolute monthly changes and use **Q3 + 1.5 × IQR**, with linearly interpolated sample quartiles. This gives 0.896758 weeks for p50, 1.215379 weeks for p92 and 93,550.5 pathways for total. The rule is based on the observed distribution and is a descriptive flag, not a statistical significance test. Counts and percentiles are screened separately.

Every revised/first-release transition is logged in `anomaly_national_discontinuities.csv`: October 2025, December 2025, January 2026, March 2026 and April 2026. The January 2024 starting row is not treated as a transition. The recurring October p92 falls and April 2024 median fall show that a large move is not unique to late first-release months. The current files alone cannot identify how much any change is a revision effect; paired first-release/revised versions would be needed.

The fixed-provider tests are in `anomaly_common_provider_sensitivity.csv`. They remove provider appearance/disappearance from each adjacent pair, while preserving the distribution submitted by surviving providers. They do not isolate treatment, reporting quality, specialty recoding, case mix or changing services.

## Reproduction and audit files

Run `scripts/anomaly_hunt.py` with the project's `.venv311/bin/python`. It consumes the built caches, then independently reads the raw sources for blank certification and count/total validity. All data checks write `anomaly_*.csv` or JSON; this report records interpretation of those checks. The official workbook comparison is an additional read-only source confirmation, not required to reconstruct the local CSV results.

`anomaly_raw_blank_certificates.csv.gz` has one record for every raw row containing blanks, including provider, commissioner, part, specialty, source, observed sum and independent residual. Its hexadecimal mask packs zero-based weekly-column blank flags with NumPy `packbits(..., bitorder='little')`; `band_count` is 53 or105 and the source header gives the column order. Decode with `np.unpackbits(np.frombuffer(bytes.fromhex(mask), dtype=np.uint8), bitorder='little')[:band_count]`. A set bit identifies a blank certified zero; an observed zero has no bit. The log is compressed only to avoid repeating a long list of band names hundreds of thousands of times.

`anomaly_recoding_candidates.csv`, `anomaly_provider_presence_events.csv` and `anomaly_provider_name_changes.csv` carry all candidate cases, sizes and suggested dispositions. The candidates are intentionally exhaustive under the stated screen, including small-count cases; none is silently removed because it looks unimportant. The scan compares all non-total sibling codes, including X codes, not just the seven selected specialties. It searches for oddities beyond the brief's examples, but cannot establish that every possible recoding follows this numeric pattern.
